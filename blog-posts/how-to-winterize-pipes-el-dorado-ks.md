## Why Pipe Winterization Matters in Butler County

If you've lived in El Dorado long enough, you know Kansas winters don't follow a script. One week it's 55°F and sunny — the next, a cold snap rolls in off the plains and overnight temps plunge into the single digits. That kind of temperature swing is exactly what causes pipes to freeze and burst.

A burst pipe isn't just an inconvenience. It can dump hundreds of gallons of water into your home before you even realize something's wrong. The water damage alone — drywall, flooring, insulation — can cost far more than the repair itself. The good news: most frozen pipe disasters are completely preventable with a little prep before the cold hits.

Here's what Butler County homeowners should do every fall to protect their plumbing.

## Step 1: Identify Your Vulnerable Pipes

Not all pipes are equally at risk. The ones most likely to freeze are:

- **Pipes in unheated spaces** — garages, crawl spaces, attics, and uninsulated basements
- **Exterior wall pipes** — plumbing routed along outside walls with little insulation between them and the cold
- **Outdoor hose bibs and irrigation lines** — these are almost always first to go
- **Lines in rarely used rooms** — guest bathrooms, vacation properties, or rooms with the heat turned down

Walk your property before the first hard freeze. Identify where your pipes run and make a mental note of any that are exposed or uninsulated.

## Step 2: Disconnect and Drain Outdoor Hoses

This is the easiest step and the one most people skip. Leaving a garden hose connected to an outdoor spigot traps water in the line. When that water freezes, it creates back-pressure that can crack the bib or the pipe behind the wall.

- Disconnect all garden hoses and drain them
- Shut off interior valves that feed outdoor hose bibs (most homes have them)
- Open the exterior spigot to let any remaining water drain out
- If you have an irrigation system, get it blown out with compressed air before the ground freezes

## Step 3: Insulate Exposed Pipes

Foam pipe insulation is cheap and available at any hardware store. It slips right over copper or PVC pipe and can make a significant difference in crawl spaces, garages, and basement areas that don't stay warm.

Pay particular attention to:

- Pipes along exterior walls in the basement
- Any plumbing in an unheated garage
- Lines that run through a crawl space with vents

For extra protection in areas that get truly cold, consider **heat tape** (also called pipe heating cable). It wraps around the pipe and plugs into an outlet, keeping the line above freezing even in extreme conditions.

## Step 4: Seal Drafts Near Pipe Runs

Cold air infiltration is a major culprit. If there's a gap around a pipe where it passes through an exterior wall or the foundation, cold air streams right in and drops the temperature around that pipe dramatically.

Use caulk or spray foam to seal any penetrations where pipes pass through exterior walls. Check your basement rim joists — those are notoriously leaky — and stuff any open gaps with insulation.

## Step 5: Know Where Your Main Shutoff Is

Even with all the right prep, freezes happen. If a pipe does burst, every second you spend looking for the main shutoff is water damage piling up on your floor.

Find your main water shutoff valve now, before winter. Make sure everyone in the household knows where it is and that it actually turns off the water. If the valve is stiff or corroded, have it serviced — a shutoff that won't budge during an emergency is useless.

## Step 6: Keep the Heat On (Even When You're Away)

If you're leaving town during a cold stretch, don't turn the heat off to save money. Set the thermostat to at least 55°F. The cost of running heat for a week is nothing compared to water damage from a burst pipe.

Opening cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls also helps — it lets the home's heat reach pipes that would otherwise sit in a cold cabinet cavity.

## When to Call a Plumber in El Dorado

If you turn on a faucet and nothing comes out during cold weather, you may already have a frozen pipe. **Do not use an open flame to thaw it.** A hair dryer or heating pad on low is safer, but if you can't locate or safely access the frozen section, call a licensed plumber before the situation turns into a full rupture.
