## Signs of a Hidden Water Leak

**Not all plumbing leaks make a splash—some do damage silently behind walls, under floors, or in your foundation.** As a homeowner in **Butler County**, it’s important to know the signs of a **hidden water leak** before it turns into a costly disaster. At **Sunflower Plumbing**, we specialize in **leak detection and plumbing repairs**, and we’re here to help you spot the warning signs early.

**Unexplained Spike in Your Water Bill** - If your water usage habits haven’t changed but your bill has skyrocketed, there’s a good chance you have a hidden leak. Even a small drip can waste hundreds of gallons of water each month. Tip: Compare your current bill to the same month last year. A sudden increase is a red flag.

**Musty Odors or Mold Growth** Leaks behind walls or under flooring can lead to moist, humid conditions, perfect for mold and mildew. If you smell mustiness or see spots on walls, ceilings, or baseboards, don’t ignore it. Tip: Mold often grows out of sight—pay attention to odors even if you can’t see visible growth.

**Stained or Warped Walls & Floors** Water can cause drywall to bubble, sag, or discolor, while hardwood and laminate floors may warp or buckle. These are telltale signs that moisture is building up where it shouldn’t be. Tip: Check around bathrooms, kitchens, and water heaters—these areas are leak-prone.

**Constantly Running Water Meter** Want a quick test? Turn off all the water in your home, then check your water meter. If it’s still spinning, you may have a leak underground or within your walls. Tip: This is one of the most accurate ways to detect a hidden leak yourself.

**Low Water Pressure or Air in the Lines** If your faucets or showers have weaker flow, or you hear hissing or bubbling when turning on water, that could indicate a leak in your supply line. Tip: Don’t just assume your pipes are old—low pressure can mean a breach somewhere in the system.

## Prevent Future Plumbing Leaks

Install a whole-home water monitoring system

Have regular plumbing inspections

Replace aging pipes, especially galvanized or polybutylene

Insulate pipes to prevent freezing in winter
