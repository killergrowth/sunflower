## A Burst Pipe Won't Wait — And Neither Should You

It's 6 a.m. You hear water running somewhere it shouldn't be. You walk into your laundry room and there's an inch of standing water on the floor. That's a burst pipe — and every second it runs is more damage to your floors, walls, and foundation.

If you're dealing with this right now in El Dorado or anywhere in Butler County, here's exactly what to do before the plumber arrives, and what Sunflower Plumbing will do to fix it right.

---

## Step 1: Shut Off the Water Immediately

Find your main water shutoff valve and turn it off. In most Kansas homes, it's located near the water meter — either in the basement, utility room, or outside near the foundation. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

If you can't find it or it won't budge, call us. We can walk you through it.

Once the water is off, open a faucet on the lowest level of your home to drain the remaining pressure from the pipes. This slows the damage while you wait for help.

---

## Step 2: Document the Damage

Before you start cleaning up, take photos and video of everything — the pipe, the water, the affected areas. You'll need this for insurance. Don't move anything until you've captured it.

---

## Step 3: Start Removing Standing Water

If you have a wet/dry shop vac or mop, start pulling water out of the space. The faster you remove standing water, the less likely you are to deal with mold or structural damage down the line. Towels work too — use whatever you've got.

---

## Why Pipes Burst in El Dorado, KS

Butler County winters can be brutal. When temperatures drop fast and stay below freezing for extended periods, pipes in uninsulated crawl spaces, exterior walls, or garages are the first to go. Kansas weather doesn't give a lot of warning — a cold front rolls in overnight and by morning you've got a split line.

Other common causes we see in El Dorado homes:

- **Aging galvanized or copper pipes** that corrode and weaken over time
- **High water pressure** creating stress fractures
- **Tree root intrusion** on main supply lines
- **Sudden pressure surges** from water hammer

If your home is older or you've had slow leaks that "came and went," don't wait for a full burst. A small repair now beats an emergency later.
